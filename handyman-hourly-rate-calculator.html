How the number is worked out

First we add your tax set-aside on top of the take-home pay you want. Then we add your yearly business costs, which gives the revenue you need. That revenue is divided by the hours you can actually bill. If you work 45 hours a week but only about 29 of them are on a job, your rate has to pay for all 45.

Where most owners go wrong

  • Pricing from an employee wage. Your rate also has to cover the truck, the insurance and the hours you spend quoting.
  • Forgetting slow seasons. Enter the weeks you really work, not 52.
  • Never raising prices. Run this again every year as your costs go up.

Questions

What share of my hours can I bill?

Many solo owners bill between 55% and 75% of the hours they work. The rest goes on driving, quoting, buying supplies and admin. Track a normal week to find your number.

Is the tax set-aside accurate?

No, it's a simple savings estimate. Your real rate depends on where you live and how your business is set up, so ask a tax professional.

Should I charge hourly or a flat price?

Either can work. Many customers prefer a flat price, so use this rate to build your flat prices and check they hold up.
